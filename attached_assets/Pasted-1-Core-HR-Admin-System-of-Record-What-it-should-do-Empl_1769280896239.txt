1) Core HR & Admin (System of Record)

What it should do

Employee master record: biodata, contacts, IDs, dependents, emergency, bank, tax, pension, skills, licenses, attachments.

Org structure & job architecture: departments, locations, cost centers, job families, grades/steps, positions, reporting lines, org chart.

Employment lifecycle: hire, confirmation/probation, transfers, promotions, acting roles, secondments, contract renewals, status changes.

Policy & compliance records: contract templates, policy acknowledgements, disciplinary actions, warnings, commendations.

Document & records management: employee file (structured + attachments), retention rules, versioning.

Best-practice note: Core HR should include Position Management / Establishment Control (especially in government) and cost center/budget tagging for every role/transaction.

2) Recruitment & Onboarding

What it should do

Requisitions: vacancy requests tied to approved positions/headcount; approvals and budget checks.

ATS: posting, sourcing, screening, scoring, interviews, panels, tests, background checks, reference checks.

Offers & hiring: offer generation, negotiations, e-sign, pre-employment docs.

Onboarding workflows: tasks for HR/IT/Finance, provisioning, policy signoffs, training assignments, probation plan.

What many systems miss: structured selection audit trail (who scored what, when, why) and fairness/EEO reporting where applicable.

3) Talent Management

This label is broad. In best practice it’s usually a suite:

What it should do

Performance management: goals/KPIs, cascades, check-ins, 360 feedback, calibration, ratings, improvement plans.

Succession planning: critical roles, readiness levels, talent pools, 9-box, development actions.

Career & development: career paths, competency frameworks, individual development plans (IDPs).

Common gap: treating “Talent” as separate from Learning (LMS) and Workforce Planning (see missing modules).

4) Compensation & Benefits

What it should do

Structures: grades/steps, pay bands, allowances, per diems, hazard pay, overtime rules.

Benefits admin: eligibility, enrollments, dependents, employer/employee contributions, vendors.

Comp planning: merit cycles, promotions, bonuses, acting/temporary pay, retro adjustments.

Total rewards statements and payslip visibility (usually via self-service).

Best-practice note: This module should produce payroll-ready inputs, even if payroll is a separate system.

5) Employee & Manager Self-Service (ESS/MSS)

What it should do

ESS: update profile (with controls), request leave, view schedules, payslips, letters, training, performance goals, submit claims.

MSS: approve requests, manage team time/leave, initiate staff actions, view team analytics, run lightweight reports.

Case/request center: “HR helpdesk” tickets (letters, corrections, disputes).

Best-practice note: Self-service isn’t a module—it’s a channel sitting on top of workflows, permissions, and audit.

6) Biometrics Punch In/Out

This should be designed as part of a broader Time & Attendance capability.

What it should do

Device management: device registry, firmware/config, site mapping, offline buffering, syncing.

Attendance rules engine: shifts, grace periods, late/early, overtime, weekends/holidays, exceptions.

Exception handling: missed punches, manual corrections with approvals, geo-fence or site validation if needed.

Anti-fraud controls: liveness checks (where supported), duplicate detection, audit trail, supervisor attestations.

Outputs: timesheets, overtime approvals, payroll inputs, compliance reports.

Common gap: biometrics without a rules engine + exception workflow becomes a data dump and creates payroll disputes.

7) Analytics & Reporting

What it should do

Operational dashboards: headcount, attendance, leave liability, vacancies, overtime, turnover.

Strategic HR analytics: workforce trends, hiring funnel, performance distribution, comp equity, succession coverage.

Regulatory/statutory reporting: tax/pension, labor reporting, audits.

Ad hoc reporting: report builder with row-level security; export; scheduled reports.

Best-practice note: analytics must respect row-level access, not just “who can open the dashboard”.

What you likely left out (must-haves for international best practice)
A) Time, Leave, Scheduling (beyond biometrics)

Even if you keep “biometrics” as the capture method, you still need:

leave types/policies, accruals, carryover, approvals

shift rosters, timesheets, overtime approvals

holiday calendars, multi-location rules

B) Payroll (or at least Payroll Interface)

An HRMIS that can’t reliably produce payroll inputs is incomplete for most organizations.

earnings/deductions elements, retro, arrears

integration/export to payroll system (or built-in payroll if that’s your product scope)

C) Learning Management (LMS) + Compliance Training

course catalog, enrollments, completions, certifications, renewals

mandatory training tracking and manager visibility

D) Employee Relations / Case Management

grievances, disciplinary cases, investigations, outcomes

incident logging (where relevant), policy breaches

structured workflow + evidence attachments + audit trail

E) Offboarding & Exit Management

resignations/terminations, clearance workflows, asset return, access removal

exit interviews and attrition reasons analytics

F) Position Control / Headcount & Budget Controls (especially government)

establishment register, approved posts, vacancy control

link staff actions to approved positions and budget lines

G) Document Management & E-Signature

templated letters/contracts, version control

e-signing and secure storage; policy acknowledgement tracking

H) Platform Governance (non-negotiable)

To meet international expectations, you need these as first-class capabilities:

RBAC/ABAC (role + attribute based access), separation of duties

Workflow engine (configurable approvals, SLAs, escalations)

Audit logs for every change (who/what/when/before-after)

Security: SSO/SAML/OIDC, MFA, encryption at rest/in transit, secrets management

Data protection: consent, retention, right-to-access/export, anonymization

Multi-tenant controls (if SaaS): tenant isolation, tenant-level configs, per-tenant encryption keys (ideal)

Backup/DR: RPO/RTO targets, immutable backups, restore drills

I) Integrations Layer

APIs + webhooks, import/export tooling, scheduled jobs

integrations: finance/FMIS, payroll, identity provider, email/SMS, devices, pension/tax, document signing

A clean “international-standard HRMIS” module map (recommended)

If you want a universal HRMIS portal that matches best practice, consider packaging like this:

Core HR (People + Org + Positions)

Recruitment (ATS) + Onboarding

Time, Leave, Scheduling + Attendance (biometrics as capture)

Performance + Succession + Career

Learning (LMS) + Compliance

Compensation & Benefits + Payroll Interface

Employee Relations (Cases/Grievances/Discipline)

Documents + Templates + E-Signature

Self-Service (ESS/MSS) as the UX layer

Analytics + Data Warehouse/Reporting

Platform: Security, Workflows, Audit, Integrations, Admin
